Mountain Window: Trail Weather by Cloudshadow Studios finds the best weather window for hiking, trail running, climbing, skiing, mountain biking, camping and paragliding, and shows what to expect and what to bring. When writing, include your iPhone (and Apple Watch) model, iOS version, the place you were looking at and a short description of what happened. A screenshot helps a lot.

Use it as one input, not the final word

Mountain Window shows computer forecasts. They can be wrong, especially in the mountains, where storms build quickly and conditions change over short distances. It doesn’t forecast avalanches, rockfall, river levels or trail closures. Before you go, check official forecasts and warnings for the area, such as your national weather service and local avalanche center, and turn back when what you see doesn’t match the forecast.

Where the forecasts come from

Forecasts, high-point temperatures and wind, terrain heights and air quality come from Open-Meteo. Official weather alerts and next-hour rain come from Apple Weather. Trails, trailheads, campsites and launch sites come from OpenStreetMap contributors, and climbing crags and route counts from OpenBeta. The app’s About screen lists every source and credit.

Near me says the forecast is from somewhere else

When you’re driving or your position is changing quickly, Mountain Window may not be able to get a fresh forecast for exactly where you are. Instead of showing nothing, it shows the nearest forecast you downloaded recently and labels how far away it was from. It only does this when that forecast is close, at a similar elevation and recent. Planning is paused until a forecast for your actual position arrives, which it fetches again automatically.

I don’t see trails or spots on the Explore map

Make sure an activity is selected in the row of chips above the map; scroll the row sideways if the one you want is off screen. Trail lines are drawn once you zoom in. Trails, trailheads, campsites, crags and launch sites currently cover the United States, with more countries to come. Elsewhere, the map shows places from Apple Maps.

A trail, crag or launch is wrong or missing

Map data comes from OpenStreetMap and climbing data from OpenBeta, both maintained by volunteers. You can fix it at the source (openstreetmap.org or openbeta.io), and the change reaches the app when we next update the map data. You can also email us.

Checking a launch for paragliding

For a launch, you can enter the wind directions it works in. Mountain Window then compares the forecast wind direction and speed with those directions. Surface forecasts are only context, not a flying decision: check aviation forecasts, the site guide, local pilots and conditions on launch.

Picking my own time to go

In the Plan tab, use the custom window to choose any start and end time in the next seven days, in 15-minute steps, in the destination’s time zone. Mountain Window shows the weather for that window and warns you about storms or other problems, and you can still save it as an outing.

Reminders and trip weather alerts

Allow notifications when asked, or turn them on in iPhone Settings → Notifications → Mountain Window. Trip weather alerts re-check the forecast for upcoming outings in the background and let you know about meaningful changes. iOS decides when background checks run, so they are a best-effort heads-up, not a warning service. Always check the forecast again before you leave.

Widgets

To add a widget, touch and hold your Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget, and search for Mountain Window. Widgets work in the default, dark, tinted and clear Home Screen styles. If a widget looks out of date, open the app once to refresh its forecast.

Apple Watch

Install Mountain Window on your Apple Watch from the Watch app on your iPhone (My Watch → Available Apps). Your places and outings come over from your iPhone. Open the app on your iPhone once if the watch shows no places.

Tips
